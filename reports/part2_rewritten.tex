\large Steps 0--4 --- methods, results and reproduction}
\author{Part II working note}

\begin{abstract}
We implemented Steps 0--4 of the Part II plan on the nine Part I benchmark cases.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item \textbf{Step 0.} Each measurement context in fact reads out a maximal group of $2^n$ commuting Pauli products. We built the tools to exploit this: measurement circuits, the moments of every group from one transform, and exact shot-level sampling. All are validated against dense linear algebra and against Part I.
  \item \textbf{Step 1.} The II-0 baseline runs Part I's estimator as a shot-level experiment, with pairwise elimination, radius schedule $\gamma=0.9$ and top-up allocation. Its median cost is 62--83\% below Part I's M3 as run. It made 5 wrong selections in 1{,}100 trials, all on the tied H$_4$ HF rows.
  \item \textbf{Step 2.} 58--75\% of the Pauli products in $\mathcal B_0$ are measured by at least two contexts, so coefficient splitting is possible everywhere.
  \item \textbf{Step 3.} With exact covariances, splitting (II-A) lowers the M3 cost by 38--80\% across all cases. Ghost Pauli products (II-B) add up to 10 points. Redesign at every elimination breakpoint (oracle II-D) adds up to 19 points, but only under top-up accounting.
  \item \textbf{Prior transfer.} A design fitted to Hartree--Fock covariances and applied to the correlated state is 66--69\% \emph{more} expensive than II-0.
  \item \textbf{Step 4.} The design is learned from the shots and the confidence radii are estimated too, so the run is fully non-oracle. It relies on cross-fitting, held-out radii and allocation, and a minimum number of shots per fold before a context is learned. On the two H$_4$ CISD rows and on LiH, II-A then costs 36--43\% less than II-0 run the same way, with no wrong selections in 500 trials. That is 75--84\% of the online oracle ceiling. The HF prior on its own is unreliable (85--99\% correct, heavy tails), and no prior improved on the data alone.
\end{itemize}
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and conventions}
\label{sec:problem}

At one ADAPT-VQE step, with state $\ket\psi$ and pool $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$, the task is to identify $i^\star=\arg\max_i|g_i|$, where
\[
  g_i=\langle C_i\rangle_\psi,\qquad C_i=[H,G_i]=\sum_\ell A_{i\ell}P_\ell ,
\]
using as few \emph{context-shots} as possible. One context-shot is one state preparation plus one measurement in one fully commuting (FC) context.

\paragraph{Cases.}
The nine Part I cases, rebuilt with the current Part I pipeline:
\begin{itemize}
  \item systems: H$_4$ square with side $1.0$ and $2.0$\,\AA; LiH at $R=3.0$\,\AA; H$_2$O at $R_{\mathrm{OH}}=0.9572$ and $1.75$\,\AA;
  \item states: HF and CISD (LiH HF only);
  \item STO-3G, Jordan--Wigner, spin-orbital UCCSD pool ($N=26,92,140$ on $8,12,14$ qubits);
  \item Part I's validation gate, and Part I's deterministic first-fit FC partition of $\mathcal B_0=\bigcup_i\supp(C_i)$ into parent contexts $\mathcal M_\alpha$.
\end{itemize}
The rebuilt $|g_i|$ agree with Part I's committed values to $3\times10^{-10}$. Three H$_4$ rows differ in the sign of some $g_i$. This is an orbital-phase convention, and every quantity reported here is invariant under it.

\paragraph{Confidence and radii.}
A confidence radius is $r=z_\delta\epsilon$, with $\epsilon$ the one-standard-error target and $z_\delta=\Phi^{-1}(1-\delta/2N)$, where $\delta=0.05$ (Bonferroni over the full pool, as in Part I). The gap is $|g_{(1)}|-|g_{(2)}|$, and $\Delta_i=|g_{(1)}|-|g_i|$.

\paragraph{Oracle versus measured.}
Steps 2--3 are oracle: exact covariances drive the designs and exact gradients fix the elimination trajectory. Their numbers are ceilings. Steps 1 and 4 sample measurement outcomes.

%=====================================================================
\section{Measurement model (Step 0)}
\label{sec:measurement}

\paragraph{Measured groups.}
A context is measured by a Clifford $U_\alpha$ followed by readout of all $n$ qubits. One shot therefore gives the eigenvalues of all $2^n$ elements of the maximal abelian group $\mathcal G_\alpha=\{\pm U_\alpha^\dagger Z^{\bm z}U_\alpha\}$. A library Pauli $P_\ell$ is \emph{measured by} $\alpha$ if $P_\ell\in\mathcal G_\alpha$, equivalently if it commutes with all $n$ generators of $\mathcal G_\alpha$.

\paragraph{Completion.}
Let $r_\alpha$ be the rank of the parent members in $\mathbb F_2^{2n}$. The generators are completed to $n$ by adding, one at a time, the first candidate that commutes with all current generators and lies outside their span. Two candidate orders are used:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{canonical}: single-qubit $Z_q$, then $X_q$, then $Y_q$;
  \item \emph{mass}: library Paulis in decreasing order of $\sum_i|A_{i\ell}|$.
\end{itemize}
If a candidate list runs out, a vector of the symplectic complement is used. A seeded \emph{random} order serves as a control. ``Span'' (no completion) is a diagnostic lower bound on overlap, not a circuit.

\paragraph{Circuit synthesis.}
The circuit is built from the binary tableau $[X|Z]$ of the $n$ generators in four steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Row-reduce $X$, and apply $H$ on the non-pivot qubits, which makes $X$ invertible.
  \item Row-reduce to $X=I$. This forces $Z$ to be symmetric.
  \item Apply $S$ on every qubit with $Z_{qq}=1$, and $CZ$ on every pair with $Z_{ab}=1$.
  \item Apply $H$ on all qubits.
\end{enumerate}
Signs are not tracked during synthesis. Instead, every Pauli of interest is propagated through the gate list with exact phases ($P=i^kX^{\bm x}Z^{\bm z}$), which gives $U_\alpha P_\ell U_\alpha^\dagger=s_\ell Z^{\bm z_\ell}$ directly.

\paragraph{Moments and sampling.}
With outcome distribution $p_\alpha(b)=|\langle b|U_\alpha|\psi\rangle|^2$, all group means come from one Walsh--Hadamard transform:
\begin{equation}
  E_\alpha[\bm z]=\sum_b p_\alpha(b)(-1)^{\bm z\cdot b},\qquad
  \mu_\ell=s_\ell E_\alpha[\bm z_\ell],\qquad
  \Cov(P_l,P_m)=s_ls_m\big(E_\alpha[\bm z_l\oplus\bm z_m]-E_\alpha[\bm z_l]E_\alpha[\bm z_m]\big).
  \label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
The exact $p_\alpha$ gives the oracle moments. A histogram of $m$ sampled outcomes gives the empirical ones, with the factor $m/(m-1)$ on the covariance. Sampling $m$ shots is a single multinomial draw over $2^n$ outcomes, whose cost does not depend on $m$.

\section{Estimators and levels}
\label{sec:levels}

For a fixed context library, every linear unbiased estimator has the form
\begin{equation}
  \hat g_i=\sum_\alpha\bm x_{i\alpha}^{\!\top}\hat{\bm\mu}_\alpha,
  \qquad
  \sum_{\alpha:\,P_\ell\in\mathcal G_\alpha}x_{i\alpha\ell}=A_{i\ell}\quad\forall\ell .
  \label{eq:abc}
\end{equation}
Let $M_q=F_{i\alpha}=\sum_\ell x_{i\alpha\ell}P_\ell$ be the fragments, let $C$ hold their coefficients, and let $B$ select each generator's fragments. Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:abc} is exactly $A=BC$. Every design is checked for $\max|A-BC|\le10^{-9}\max|A|$ and saved as the pair $(B,C)$.

The levels differ only in which coordinates $(\alpha,\ell)$ may be non-zero:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.4em,labelwidth=3em]
  \item[II-0] $\ell\in\supp(C_i)$ in its Part I parent context only. This is Part I's estimator; nothing is free.
  \item[II-A] $\ell\in\supp(C_i)$ in every context that measures it (coefficient splitting).
  \item[II-B] II-A, plus Paulis with $A_{i\ell}=0$ that are measured by at least two contexts already carrying $C_i$ (zero-sum ghosts). Candidates come from the rest of the library, i.e.\ other generators' supports. They are capped at 200 per generator, ranked by the single-context control-variate score $\sum_\alpha\Cov(F^{(0)}_{i\alpha},P_\ell)^2/\Var P_\ell$, where $F^{(0)}$ is the II-0 fragment.
\end{description}
Coefficients are post-processing. Any design satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:abc} can be applied to every shot already taken.

%=====================================================================
\section{Allocation, trajectories and cost metrics}
\label{sec:costs}

\paragraph{Allocation.}
With $n_\alpha$ shots per context, $\Var\hat g_i=\sum_\alpha q_{i\alpha}/n_\alpha$, where $q_{i\alpha}=\bm x_{i\alpha}^{\!\top}\Sigma_\alpha\bm x_{i\alpha}$ is the one-shot fragment variance. The allocation solves
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\bm n}\sum_\alpha n_\alpha\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\Var\hat g_i\le\epsilon^2\ \ \forall i\in\mathcal A,\qquad n_\alpha\ge c_\alpha .
  \label{eq:alloc}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item With $c=0$ this is Part I's solver, reused unchanged.
  \item With committed shots $c\neq0$ (``top-up'') it is solved by multiplicative dual ascent. The minimiser of the Lagrangian is $n_\alpha=\max\!\big(c_\alpha,\sqrt{\sum_i\lambda_iq_{i\alpha}}\big)$, updated by $\lambda_i\leftarrow\lambda_i(\Var_i/\epsilon^2)^{1/2}$. A final one-dimensional search scales the result to the smallest feasible allocation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cost metrics.}
All three reuse Part I's trajectories.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=5.8em,labelwidth=5.4em]
  \item[M1] One allocation over all $N$ arms at $r=\mathrm{gap}/2$.
  \item[M3 bound] Part I's exact-limit M3. The breakpoints are the distinct $\Delta_i$; at each, arms with $\Delta_i\le2r$ are active and resolved to the common radius $r=\Delta/2$.
  \item[M3 actual] Part I's event-driven M3. An arm is eliminated when $|g_j|-z\sqrt{V_j}>|g_i|+z\sqrt{V_i}$ on its \emph{actual} variance, with each event found by bisection. This is the primary metric.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Accounting over breakpoints.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Maxima} (Part I): each breakpoint is solved from scratch, and each context is charged its largest request.
  \item \emph{Top-up}: each breakpoint solves Eq.~\eqref{eq:alloc} with $c$ equal to the shots already committed.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Designs along a trajectory.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Static}: one design, optimised for the full pool, is kept throughout.
  \item \emph{Redesign} (oracle II-D): the design is re-optimised for the active set at every breakpoint.
\end{itemize}

\section{Joint design solver (Step 3)}
\label{sec:solver}

\paragraph{Problem.}
For an active set $\mathcal A$ the design problem is
\[
  \min_{\bm x,\bm n}\ \sum_\alpha n_\alpha
  \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_\alpha q_{i\alpha}(\bm x)/n_\alpha\le\epsilon^2\ \ (i\in\mathcal A),\ \ \text{Eq.~\eqref{eq:abc}}.
\]
It is jointly convex, since each term is quadratic-over-linear. Because it is scale-free, the optimal $\bm x$ does not depend on $\epsilon$.

\paragraph{Why not alternate.}
One could alternate exact per-generator designs at fixed $\bm n$ (a linear solve over the free directions) with Part I's allocation. This is iterative coefficient splitting (ICS), and it is monotone but stalls. It is iteratively reweighted least squares on a sum of norms: once a context reaches $n_\alpha=0$, no coefficient can return to it. On the winner of H$_4$ side 1.0 HF, ICS ends at 2.590 after 2{,}000 iterations, against an optimum of 2.3766 (in units of $\epsilon^{-2}$).

\paragraph{Saddle form.}
Eliminating $\bm n$ gives
\[
  \min_{\bm x}\max_{\bm\lambda\ge0}\ \sum_\alpha h(Q_\alpha)-\epsilon^2\sum_i\lambda_i,
  \qquad Q_\alpha=\sum_i\lambda_iq_{i\alpha},
  \qquad h(Q)=n+Q/n,\ \ n=\max(c_\alpha,\sqrt Q).
\]
It is solved with two nested loops:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Inner}: L-BFGS (memory 10, at most 300 iterations) over the free coefficients of all active arms at once. The objective is smoothed by $Q\to Q+\delta^2$, with $\delta$ reduced by a factor $0.3$ per outer iteration, from $0.3$ to $10^{-7}$ of the typical context norm.
  \item \emph{Outer}: 20 multiplicative dual steps per iteration. The loop stops when the exact cost has not improved by $10^{-7}$ (relative) for 8 iterations.
\end{itemize}
The reported cost is always the exact allocation of the returned design, so it is feasible whether or not the solver has converged.

\paragraph{Validation.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The single-generator optimum matches an independent smoothed L-BFGS minimisation to $10^{-4}$.
  \item Small joint instances match SLSQP to $2\times10^{-4}$.
  \item The analytic two-context ghost example is reproduced to $10^{-9}$.
  \item Optimised fragment variances match a state-vector computation to $10^{-10}$.
\end{itemize}
On the large problems the solver's optimality is not certified.

\section{Online experiment (Steps 1 and 4)}
\label{sec:online}

\paragraph{Loop.}
This is Part I's successive elimination, starting at $r=\max_i|g_i|$ with $r\leftarrow\gamma r$ each round and stopping when one arm remains or $r<10^{-3}\max_i|g_i|$. Each round does the following:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Allocate shots for the active arms at $\epsilon=r/z_\delta$ (maxima or top-up), rounded up to integers.
  \item Draw one multinomial histogram per context for the new shots.
  \item Estimate $\hat g$ from the sample means.
  \item Eliminate arms, by one of two rules:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{marginal}: Part I's Eq.~(4);
    \item \emph{pairwise}: $i$ is dropped if some $j$ has $|\hat g_j|-|\hat g_i|>z_\delta\sqrt{V_{ij}}$, where $V_{ij}=\Var(\hat g_i-s_{ij}\hat g_j)$ and $s_{ij}=\operatorname{sign}(\hat g_i\hat g_j)$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
Every context that carries a fragment receives at least one shot, because deterministic outcomes still have to be observed once. In Step 1 the radii use the oracle fragment (co)variances, as Part I does.

\paragraph{Learned designs (Step 4).}
The loop above is run with II-A coefficients learned from the data, as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Covariance model.} Per context and fold, $\widetilde\Sigma=\lambda\Sigma^{\rm prior}+(1-\lambda)\widehat\Sigma^{\rm emp}$ with $\lambda=\nu/(\nu+m)$, where $m$ is the number of shots. $\widehat\Sigma^{\rm emp}$ is the unbiased sample covariance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:moments}. It is positive semidefinite, and so is every shrinkage of it, so no projection is needed. The priors are the HF determinant (in the CISD problem's own orbital basis), none ($\nu=0$), or the oracle (a diagnostic).
  \item \emph{Cross-fitting.} Every context's shots are split into two folds. The design fitted on fold A (an exact per-generator solve at fold A's shots) is applied to fold B's means, and vice versa. The two estimates are averaged. Each half-estimate is unbiased whatever its design.
  \item \emph{Minimum data.} A context may carry learned coefficients only when both folds hold at least 50 shots in it. Elsewhere a generator keeps its II-0 coefficients.
  \item \emph{Guard.} A fitted design is kept for generator $i$ only if the other fold's covariance predicts a lower variance than the II-0 coefficients do. Otherwise $i$ falls back to II-0.
  \item \emph{Schedule.} The first round is an II-0 pilot. Designs are refitted whenever the shots held have grown by a factor of 1.5.
  \item \emph{Radii.} Either estimated or oracle (the latter to isolate the design effect). Estimated radii use only the \emph{held-out} fold's covariance: design $\bm x^f$ is evaluated with $\widetilde\Sigma^{\bar f}$, which is independent of it. For the cross-fitted estimate,
  \[
    \Cov(\hat g_i,\hat g_j)=\tfrac14\sum_{f}\sum_\alpha\bm x^{f\top}_{i\alpha}\widetilde\Sigma^{\bar f}_\alpha\bm x^{f}_{j\alpha}/n^{\bar f}_\alpha ,
  \]
  where $\bar f$ is the fold not used to fit $\bm x^f$.
  \item \emph{Allocation.} Shots are planned with exactly the per-shot variance the radii will use, $\tfrac12\sum_f\bm x^{f\top}_{i\alpha}\widetilde\Sigma^{\bar f}_\alpha\bm x^f_{i\alpha}$ per context, and topped up from the shots already held.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Three failures of the first Step 4 implementation.}
Each was found on LiH or H$_4$ side 2.0 and is now covered by a test or a diagnostic.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Learning from too little data.} Fitting straight after the pilot round used covariances from one or two samples per fold. Their spurious zero-variance directions were exploited by the design and the radii alike. On LiH, 46 of 100 trials eliminated almost every arm in round 2, and only 54\% selected the winner. Fix: the minimum-data rule.
  \item \emph{Radii from a covariance that includes the fitting fold.} A design's variance was estimated from both folds, one of which it was fitted to. Fix: held-out radii. A test checks that the estimated variance matches the actual spread of the estimate across replicates (0.8--1.35).
  \item \emph{Planning and radii that disagreed.} Planning used one fold's design and covariance, while the radii used both. On H$_4$ side 2.0, a context whose 5-shot fold happened to show zero variance was never topped up, while the other fold kept a variance there that the radii could not shrink. Two trials ran to the radius floor, at $7.8\times10^8$ shots each, against a median of $1.3\times10^4$. Fix: plan with the radii's own variance.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Observations.}
\begin{itemize}

\paragraph{Findings.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Learning from the data recovers 75--84\% of the ceiling, fully non-oracle, with no wrong selections.} II-A with covariances estimated from the shots costs 36\%, 43\% and 39\% less than II-0 run the same way. The online oracle ceiling is 43\%, 55\% and 52\%. All 500 trials selected the winner. The data-only rows have no tail: the 90th percentiles are 36{,}052, 20{,}382 and 228{,}603.
  \item \emph{Estimating variances is nearly free at II-0.} Estimated and oracle radii differ by at most 6\% (within noise), with no wrong selections.
  \item \emph{The HF prior alone fails twice over.}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Its design is wrong for the correlated state.
      \item Its near-zero variances corrupt the radii (85--99\% correct, heavy tails; medians 32{,}540 and 25{,}448).
      \item They also corrupt the allocation: with oracle radii, runs stall at the radius floor ($10^8$--$10^9$ shots).
    \end{itemize}
  \item \emph{A prior helps only with little weight, and never beat the data alone here.} With the HF prior, $\nu=100$ is 3--6 points worse than data alone, and $\nu=1000$ is worse still, with 5 wrong selections on side 2.0. On LiH the flat prior at $\nu=100$ is 3 points worse.
  \item \emph{The guard rarely acts once the minimum-data rule is in place.} With and without it the results agree within noise. It is kept as a safety net. Its apparent importance in an earlier run came from the planning mismatch (failure 3 in Sec.~\ref{sec:online}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The guard chooses between two unbiased estimators using the evaluation fold's second moments, which could bias the estimate at second order. A fixed-shot test finds no bias above $4\sigma$ over 150 replicates, and estimated variances within 0.8--1.35 of the actual spread.
  \item Designs are refitted per generator at fixed shots, not with the joint solver, so the learned costs are upper bounds on what a jointly learned design would achieve.
  \item The minimum-data threshold (50 shots per fold) is a single, untuned choice.
  \item H$_2$O has not been run in Step 4 yet; see Sec.~\ref{sec:reproduction} for sizing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions and next steps}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The shot-level II-0 baseline (pairwise rule, $\gamma=0.9$, top-up) is 62--83\% cheaper than Part I's M3 as run (medians), and it is the reference for every Part II level. It made 5 wrong selections in 1{,}100 trials, all on the tied H$_4$ HF rows.
  \item Overlap is abundant, and the oracle ceiling of coefficient splitting is large: 38--80\% on M3 actual across the cases computed.
  \item Ghost Pauli products add little. Per-breakpoint redesign pays on depth-dominated rows, but only with top-up accounting.
  \item The Hartree--Fock covariance is harmful as a design prior.
  \item Learned from the shots, II-A keeps 75--84\% of its ceiling without any oracle input: $-36\%$, $-43\%$ and $-39\%$ against II-0 on the two H$_4$ CISD rows and LiH, with no wrong selections and no cost tail. It needs three safeguards:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item cross-fitting;
    \item radii and allocation computed on the held-out fold;
    \item a minimum number of shots per fold before a context is learned.
  \end{itemize}
  The default should be data-driven covariances. A prior gave no benefit here and should never be used alone.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Next.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Broaden Step 4.} Run it on the H$_2$O CISD rows (cluster; Sec.~\ref{sec:reproduction}). Swap the per-generator refit for the joint solver, and tune the minimum-data threshold.
  \item \emph{Learned redesign (II-D).} Refit for the active set at each elimination, with top-up accounting, on the depth-dominated rows, where the oracle adds up to 19 points.
  \item \emph{Correlated trajectory states.} Replace the CISD proxy with saved ADAPT trajectory states (Part I's open item), and study the prior weight $\nu$ there.
  \item \emph{Price the circuits.} The mass completion uses 8--32 CZ gates per context. Compare against a completion constrained by circuit depth.
  \item \emph{Leave II-B, II-C and II-E for later.} Ghost Pauli products added little at the oracle level.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproduction}
\label{sec:reproduction}

\paragraph{Code.}
\code{part2\_estimator\_design/} on branch \code{part2}. It imports Part I's \code{src/} directly and builds problems into its own \code{.cache/}.

\paragraph{Environment.}
WSL Ubuntu, Python 3.12.3, with the versions pinned in \code{requirements.txt}: NumPy 2.5.2, SciPy 1.18.1, PySCF 2.14.0, OpenFermion 1.8.1. NumPy $\ge2$ is required. \code{part2\_estimator\_design/Dockerfile} builds the same environment from the repository root. It is not yet tested, since no Docker was available here. On a cluster, either convert it with Apptainer or create a venv from \code{requirements.txt}.

\paragraph{Modules} (\code{src/}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{symplectic}, \code{clifford}: Pauli algebra, completion and circuits;
  \item \code{contexts}: context library and census;
  \item \code{sampler}: moments and sampling;
  \item \code{design}: levels and the joint solver;
  \item \code{allocation}: top-up allocation;
  \item \code{planning}: M1 and M3 trajectories;
  \item \code{online}: Step 1 loop;
  \item \code{learning}: Step 4;
  \item \code{parallel}: trial parallelism and sharding.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Commands}
(from \code{part2\_estimator\_design/}; set \code{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS=1} when using \code{--workers}):
\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/build_problems.py                        # 9 cases, checked against Part I
python -m pytest tests -q                               # 53 tests
python scripts/step0_validate.py --low-shot 0.001       # H4 sampler gate
python scripts/step0_validate.py --cases LiH_R3p0_HF --replicates 400 \
       --out runs/step0_lih
python scripts/step1_online_baseline.py --trials 250 --workers 4 --cases <H4 cases>
python scripts/step1_online_baseline.py --trials 100 --workers 8 --cases LiH_R3p0_HF
python scripts/step1_online_baseline.py --gates-only    # exact noiseless gate
python scripts/step2_overlap_census.py                  # all nine cases
python scripts/step3_oracle_ceiling.py --cases <H4 cases> --redesign
python scripts/step3_oracle_ceiling.py --cases LiH_R3p0_HF \
       --strategies mass canonical
python scripts/step3_oracle_ceiling.py --cases <H2O cases> --strategies mass \
       --levels II-0 II-A
python scripts/step3_prior_transfer.py
python scripts/step4_learned_designs.py --trials 200 --workers 4   # H4 CISD rows
python scripts/step4_learned_designs.py --cases LiH_R3p0_HF --trials 100 --workers 10
python scripts/aggregate_results.py
\end{verbatim}
For a cluster, split any Step 1 or Step 4 run with \code{--shard K/N}, one job per \code{K}. Then run \code{scripts/merge\_trials.py --step step4 --cases <case>}. It checks that every trial is present exactly once and writes the same summary file as an unsharded run.

\paragraph{Outputs.}
Each script writes \code{runs/<case>/<case>\_<step>.csv}, with a \code{*\_meta.json} recording package versions and the git commit. Steps 1 and 4 also keep every trial in \code{runs/<case>/trials/}. Step 3 designs are saved as \code{*\_B.npz} and \code{*\_C.npz}; these are not tracked and are regenerated by the script.

\paragraph{Seeds.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Step 0: seed 0.
  \item Step 1: seed 1 for Part II, and seed 0 for Part I's surrogate.
  \item Step 4: seed 2.
\end{itemize}
In Steps 1 and 4, trial $i$ uses \code{SeedSequence(seed).spawn(n)[i]}, so results do not depend on \code{--workers} or \code{--shard} (tested).

\paragraph{Runtimes and memory}
(one desktop, 8 cores/16 threads, 15\,GB):
\begin{itemize}
  \item Tests: about 3\,min.
  \item Step 3, H$_4$ with redesign: 15--20\,min per case. LiH: 5--15\,min per design. H$_2$O: 12--47\,min per design.
  \item Step 4, H$_4$: 2--5\,min per configuration (200 trials, 4 workers). The prior-only configuration with oracle radii takes 8--25\,min, because its runs stall until the radius floor.
  \item Step 4, LiH: 13--24\,min per configuration (100 trials, 10 workers, about 1.5\,GB per worker). A sparse direct solve in the design refit made LiH 8$\times$ faster than the original conjugate-gradient solve.
  \item Step 4, H$_2$O eq.\ CISD, one trial: II-0 takes 329\,s at a 3.6\,GB peak, and II-A (data only) 685\,s at a 7.1\,GB peak. Five configurations $\times$ 100 trials is therefore about 60--80 CPU-hours per H$_2$O case. Memory is the constraint, at about 7\,GB per process. For example, a 64-core, 256\,GB node runs about 32 processes and finishes one case in roughly 5 hours. Plan the cluster jobs as \code{--shard K/N} with \code{--workers} set by memory.
\end{itemize}
